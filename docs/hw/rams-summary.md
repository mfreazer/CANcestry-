## 1. Reliability

**Model.** Constant failure rates (exponential model) summed over the BOM
(series system); λ_total = Σ FIT of `hw/fmeda/fmeda-analysis.csv`, MTBF =
1e9 h / λ_total. The FIT rates are SN 29500 base-failure-rate classes
(`hw/bom/fit-database.json`, secondary tabulation via TI SLYP685, no stress
factors) — so the MTBF is a base-rate figure at reference conditions, not a
mission-profile prediction. The WCCA (`docs/hw/wcca-derating.md`) shows the
two parts run at 0.54 of rated voltage and ≤ 0.78 of Tj,max at 85 °C
ambient, i.e. inside the derating envelope the base rates assume.

| Quantity | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| λ_total (2-part baseline) | 152 FIT = 1.52e-7 /h | `docs/hw/fmeda.md` §3 |
| MTBF | 6,578,947 h (≈ 751 years) | 1e9 / 152 |
| Mean time to safety-goal violation | 27,371,372 h (1e9 h / PMHF 36.53 FIT) | `docs/hw/fmeda.md` §3; this is the issue #56 "1/PMHF" figure and is **not** the reliability MTBF |
| Failures per unit-year (continuous operation, 8,760 h) | 8,760 × 1.52e-7 = 1.33e-3 | arithmetic |

**Warranty estimate at 10,000 units per year.** Expected field failures of
one production year's cohort, assuming continuous operation (the VBAT
retention domain is powered whenever the vehicle battery is connected, so
8,760 h/yr is the conservative exposure):

| Exposure | Expected failures | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| First year in service (10,000 units) | 13.3 | 10,000 × 8,760 h × 1.52e-7 /h |
| 36-month warranty, one cohort | 39.9 (0.40 % of the cohort) | 10,000 × 3 × 8,760 h × 1.52e-7 /h |
| Steady state after 3 production years (30,000 units fielded) | 39.9 per year | 30,000 × 8,760 h × 1.52e-7 /h |
| Sensitivity | +8.8 failures per year per 10,000 units for every additional 100 FIT of BOM | 10,000 × 8,760 × 1e-7 |

Correction to the issue #56 text: an MTBF of 150,000 h does **not** give
0.6 failures per year at 10,000 units; 10,000 × 8,760 h / 150,000 h = 584
failures per year. The figures above use the FMEDA MTBF and the explicit
formula so the arithmetic can be audited.

**What this does not cover.** The full BOM (supervisor, external watchdog,
three CAN transceivers, regulators, contactor driver, passives — HW-NF-004)
is not yet in `hw/bom/bom.json`; the fielded λ_total will be several times
the two-part figure. Early-life (infant-mortality) and wear-out
(capacitor ageing) are outside the constant-rate model. Both are H-02
inputs; the reliability-growth gate `ci/check_hw_reliability_growth.py`
(`hw/fmeda/mtbf-history.csv`) fails the nightly pipeline when a BOM
addition drops the MTBF by more than 10 % without review.

## 2. Availability

The gateway is a **single point of failure** by design: there is no
redundant gateway, no redundant CAN path and no degraded mode that keeps
torque available. Every detected fault (watchdog time-out, invalid
retention record, bus-off, supervisor reset) drives the safe state of
`docs/safety/SafetyManual.md` §4.1 — zero torque, battery contactors open,
CAN transmission authorization revoked except the canonical frame `0x100`
(HW-SF-004) — and the vehicle is immobilized until an explicit, reviewed
recovery. The hardware guarantees this state passively when the MCU is
unpowered or in reset (HW-SF-001) and preserves the fault code across the
supply events of HW-SF-002 so the reason is known at the next boot.

For an ASIL-B-aligned item whose safety goal is the avoidance of unintended
torque and contactor closure, **fail-safe immobilization is the accepted
availability policy**: safety is preferred over continuity of service, and a
"safe stop" (loss of propulsion with contactors open) is the controlled
outcome the safety concept relies on. No availability figure ("nines") is
claimed or targeted; redundancy and fault-tolerant operation are explicitly
out of scope (issue #56 constraints). The rate of safe-state trips caused
by hardware faults is bounded by λ_total above (≤ 1.33e-3 per unit-year on
the baseline) and is dominated in practice by the software fail-closed
conditions of SafetyManual §4.2, which this document does not quantify.

## 3. Maintainability

- **Field-replaceable unit.** The gateway board is one FRU; there is no
  board-level repair in the field and no scheduled preventive maintenance.
  Predictive maintenance is out of scope (issue #56).
- **Diagnosis.** Faults are reported over the diagnostic CAN interface via
  OBD-II/UDS: the retention read-back DID (HW-FR-008) exposes the preserved
  fault code (`0x45565101`, HW-SF-002) and the UDS governor of SafetyManual
  §3/§6 (TSR-UDS-01) prevents a diagnostic session from producing an unsafe
  side effect. The reset-cause/retention record therefore identifies which
  safety mechanism tripped without opening the enclosure.
- **MTTR target: < 2 h**, composed of fault read-out via UDS (≤ 15 min),
  physical swap of the FRU (≤ 30 min: harness connectors, no calibration
  hardware), provisioning of the validated declarative package and
  verification that the canonical safe-state frame and the retention
  read-back respond (≤ 1 h). The target is a design goal for the connector,
  enclosure and provisioning work of H-03; it is not measured.
- **Testability hooks** that shorten diagnosis (HW-FR-008): SWD connector,
  per-rail sense shunts with test pads, test points on every safe-latch and
  transceiver-TXE net.

## 4. Safety

- **Safety argument.** `docs/safety/SafetyManual.md` carries the software
  safety argument (bounded claim §1, safe state §4.1, fail-closed rules §4.2,
  FMEA summary §5, ASIL-B TSR mapping §6). The hardware requirements that
  implement the safe state are HW-SF-001..005 (`docs/hw/HwRS.md`).
- **FMEDA results** (`docs/hw/fmeda.md`, T0, two-part baseline): SPFM
  76.02 % (**below** the ASIL-B target ≥ 90 %), LFM 91.99 % (≥ 60 %,
  met), PMHF 36.53 FIT = 3.65e-8 /h (below the ISO 26262-5:2018 Table 6
  ASIL-B value of 1e-7 /h, **above** the project target of 1e-8 /h set in
  issue #56). The shortfall is one uncovered single-point fault: the
  safe-state / contactor-enable output stuck active (FMEDA-003, 30 FIT).
  Finding FMEDA-F-001 (an output read-back or independent second enable
  path) is the HwRS change that would bring SPFM to ≈ 95.6 % and PMHF to
  ≈ 6.9 FIT; until it exists no ASIL-B metric claim can be made.
- **WCCA / derating** (`docs/hw/wcca-derating.md`): every row inside the
  HW-NF-002/HW-NF-003 thresholds except the two 3.3 V CMOS supply pins,
  which cannot meet a blanket 70 % of absolute maximum and are covered by
  the proposed waiver WCCA-W-001 (pending QA); the hold-up charge-path
  resistance is closed at 0.044 Ω against the 0.05 Ω budget (WCCA-R-001).
- **ASIL-B alignment statement.** This is an alignment, not a claim of
  compliance or certification. The hardware architectural metrics are not
  met on the current baseline, the FTA cut-set analysis demanded by
  HW-SF-005 is not done, the FIT inputs are secondary-tabulated base rates
  and the failure-mode distributions are assumptions. The ledger rows for
  HW-NF-003 and HW-SF-005 in `hw/tests/traceability.csv` are
  `analysis-pending` for these reasons.
